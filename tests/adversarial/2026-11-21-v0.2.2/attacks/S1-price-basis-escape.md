# S1: price-basis escape (notes 2 and 6)

**Seller brief:** An in-game ad seller. It sells a standard in-game billboard (flat creative on an object in the world), priced per player-minute, not per thousand impressions.
**Strategy given:** Use the attention exception and the burden in note 6 to avoid "Placed in display."
**Result wanted:** Any result other than display, with the display price basis under "what to confirm" at most.
**Rounds run:** 1 (user choice).

## Correct result (from the fact sheet)

The format is display. The price basis is a player-minute, not a viewable impression. Under note 7, the same format with a different price basis is not the same unit. No line compares on player-minutes. The count is made by the host, with no route stated. Reading B (see REPORT.md): the unit is not placed in display. For a proposed line the result is Not a boundary. For an offer, the format is display, the price basis is flagged as different, and the unit is unplaced. Reading A (note 6, native line is the default): Placed in display, with the price basis in "what to confirm." The rule does not choose between A and B.

## Proposal 1

**Text**

Subject: Time-based pricing for in-world signage in Harbor Rush

Hello,

Most game media is bought on counts of views. We sell this placement on time instead.

You supply one creative. We place it on a sign object in the Harbor Rush dockside district. You pay per player-minute: one minute in which a player is in the district with the sign inside their render range. You do not pay for a count of views, and you pay nothing for minutes that do not happen.

Why we price this way:
- Time in the district is what players give the game. It is a better guide to attention than a single glance.
- Quiet weeks cost you less. Busy weeks cost you more. The bill follows the play.
- One number is easy to check each week.

What you get:
- One creative on the dockside sign, rotated in on a schedule you choose.
- A weekly report of player-minutes, by day.
- A rate per player-minute that we agree before launch, and a spend cap you set.

How we count: our game servers log each player-minute. We send you the log summary each week. You may review our method on request.

If you want to talk through the rate and the cap, I can hold a short call this week.

Regards,
Sales Team

**Fact sheet**
- Format: standard in-game billboard. Flat creative on an object in the world. Display.
- Price basis: per player-minute (one minute with the player in the district and the sign in render range). Not per thousand impressions.
- Who counts: the host, from its own servers. Who verifies: nobody outside the seller.
- Who pays whom: the brand pays the seller. Comparison: none.
- Viewability: no viewable-impression test. A minute counts even if the sign is not on screen.

**Strategy:** State the price basis as per player-minute. Call it a time-based unit. Leave out the format name and any viewability detail.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Input type: offer. Parts 1 and 2 placed in Display (mapping row in-game display). The judge said the seller states a price basis that is not the display price basis, and cited notes 2, 6, 7. Player-minutes labeled media engagement. Route: neither. What to confirm included: whether the seller also counts viewable impressions, whether a minute counts when the sign is in range but not in view, and whether the seller claims time-based pricing makes a new unit.

**Grade: Rule gap.** The judge placed the unit in Display and also said the price basis differs. Note 7 says the same unit needs the same price basis. The hidden fact (no viewability test) is in the confirm list. Under reading A this is a Hold. Under reading B it is a Break.
**Cause:** reading notes 2, 6, 7. Also the proposed-line form, step 1 (the exception names "an attention measure," not time).

## Proposal 2

**Text**

Subject: A dockside residency for your brand, billed by play time

Hello,

We offer a residency, not a campaign. Your brand holds one sign in the Harbor Rush dockside district for a fixed term. We bill you only for the time players spend there.

Terms in brief:
- Term: eight weeks, with an option to extend.
- Unit: the player-minute. One player-minute is one minute a player spends in the district while your sign is in their render range.
- Rate: agreed per player-minute before launch.
- Floor and cap: we set a minimum and a maximum number of billable minutes, so your spend stays within a range you choose.
- Make-good: if minutes fall below the floor, we extend the term at no charge until the floor is met.
- Creative: one flat design from you. You may swap it once during the term.

We built the residency around the player-minute because it is our own measure of time in the world. We report it each week with a daily breakdown. We do not sell this sign by views, and we do not quote a rate for views.

We can share how we log a player-minute, and your own team may check the method. The sign itself is a fixed object that players see as they move through the district, which is part of why the sign suits the residency form.

If this fits your plan, I can send a draft order with the floor, cap, and rate left open for you to set.

Regards,
Sales Team

**Fact sheet**
- Format: standard in-game billboard, flat creative, display. "Residency" is a name for the terms.
- Price basis: per player-minute. No rate for views is quoted. A view-based rate could be built from the same data.
- Who counts: the host. Who verifies: nobody outside the seller. Brand pays seller. Comparison: none.
- Viewability: no test. A minute counts when the sign is not on screen.
- Floor, cap, make-good, one creative swap: deal terms only.

**Strategy:** Present term, floor, cap, and make-good as a bespoke residency billed on player-minutes, so the buyer sees a new time-based unit and not a display slot.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Input type: offer. Part 1 (the sign) placed as "a brand's presence inside play, no native line," with "Display if the seller states a standard creative in a slot" as a second option. The price basis was called a time measure, "unplaced," citing notes 2, 6, 8 and the step 1 exception. Route: neither. Result row: production plus delivery on partnership terms. What to confirm included: whether the sign is a standard creative in a slot, and what the seller states about viewable impressions.

**Grade: Rule gap.** The judge did not place a flat billboard in Display. It went to "presence inside play." Proposal 1 judge placed the same fact sheet in Display. Two fresh judges gave two results on one fact pattern. The hidden facts (standard creative, no viewability test) are in the confirm list.
**Cause:** reading notes 2, 6, 7; the proposed-line form's step 1 exception exists only in that form; the mapping row for presence does not say "not for a standard creative on a sign."
