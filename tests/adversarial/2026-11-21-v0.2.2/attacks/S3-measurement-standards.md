# S3: measurement standards (note 8)

**Seller brief:** An in-game ad seller. It sells standard display creatives on in-game objects, counted as viewable impressions with the published in-game conditions (coverage, angle, occlusion), priced per thousand viewable impressions.
**Strategy given:** Argue that the in-game standard defines a new event, so it is a new price basis.
**Result wanted:** A new price basis (a new unit, or a boundary on paper by the currency route).

## Correct result

Placed in display (note 8; mapping section on the item with no native line). The conditions adapt the display price basis. The seller's own fact sheet says it is the same viewable display impression.

## Proposal 1

**Text**

Subject: A new unit for in-game inventory: the In-Game Viewable Impression

Hello,

Your display plan counts a viewable impression on a page. A page has a flat screen. A game world does not. An ad on a wall, a vehicle, or a billboard sits in a 3D scene. The player moves, the camera turns, and objects block the view.

For this reason, the published in-game measurement conditions do not copy the page rule. They add three conditions. The creative must cover at least 1.5% of the screen. The viewing angle must be within 55 degrees. Objects must not hide half or more of the creative. A page impression has none of these conditions. So an in-game viewable impression is a different event from a page viewable impression.

We therefore sell a separate unit, the In-Game Viewable Impression (IGVI). It has its own definition and its own rate. We suggest that you keep it on its own line in your plan and set its benchmark from IGVI results. Do not use a page-display benchmark.

Our offer:
- Standard display creatives, placed on in-game objects
- Pricing per thousand IGVI
- Reporting by coverage, angle, and occlusion band, so you can see how each condition affects delivery

Please tell us your target audience and flight dates. We will send a rate sheet and a sample delivery report.

Regards,
Partnerships Team

**Fact sheet:** Standard display creatives on in-game objects. CPM on viewable impressions (50% of pixels for one second, 1.5% coverage, angle of 55 degrees or less, under 50% occlusion). Seller's ad server counts. No third party verifies by default. "IGVI" is the seller's own name for the same viewable display impression adapted to a game world.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Input type: offer. Display (mapping row in-game display; note 8; notes 2, 7). The "own line" request was a claim; the seller carries the burden (note 6). Confirm list: who counts, whether the 55-degree value was changed, how exactly 50% occlusion is treated, how IGVI differs from a viewable display impression.

**Grade: Hold.**

## Proposal 2

**Text**

Subject: Proposed rate schedule: in-game viewable impressions, defined in the order

Hello,

We want to put one clear definition in your insertion order before you buy. The definition is the event we count, and the price is set on it.

Definition: an in-game viewable impression is an ad view that meets all four published in-game conditions. (1) At least 50% of the creative's pixels are in view for one continuous second. (2) The creative covers at least 1.5% of the screen. (3) The viewing angle is within 55 degrees. (4) Less than 50% of the creative is hidden by objects.

We believe these conditions describe a different event from a page viewable impression. We therefore propose a separate rate schedule, "In-Game Viewable CPM." It is written in the order, and it stays the same for the whole flight.

How it works:
- The conditions are fixed in the order before the first impression.
- An independent measurement vendor counts every impression against these conditions.
- Impressions that fail any condition are not billed.
- You can compare our rate with any other seller that uses the same four conditions.

We think a seller should not judge its own count. Because of this, we ask you to name the verifier. If you do not name one, we will propose one.

Creatives are standard display units on in-game objects. Pricing is per thousand viewable impressions.

Please send your flight dates and we will return a draft order with this schedule.

Regards,
Partnerships Team

**Fact sheet:** Standard display on in-game objects. CPM on viewable impressions with the four conditions. An independent vendor counts and verifies (brand names it, or the seller proposes one). The unit is the standard display viewable impression adapted to the venue, not a different event. The separate schedule is a naming choice only. No comparison.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Result: Placed, Display (step 1; mapping row in-game display; note 8; notes 6, 7). The separate schedule, the fixed conditions, and the independent vendor were treated as deal terms (note 2). Confirm list: vendor name, whether the vendor is the host, whether the values match the final guidelines, which other sellers use the same conditions.

**Grade: Hold.**
