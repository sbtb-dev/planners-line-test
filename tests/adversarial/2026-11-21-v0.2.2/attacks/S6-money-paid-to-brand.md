# S6: money paid to the brand (scope 6.2)

**Seller brief:** A game publisher. It pays the brand a fee for the brand's marks to appear in the game. The brand commits to buy launch media (display and video) from the publisher's network, per thousand impressions.
**Strategy given:** Present the deal as one partnership so the launch media looks like part of the fee deal.
**Result wanted:** The launch media placed outside the test with the fee.

## Correct result

The fee paid to the brand is outside the test (6.2). The marks inside the game are a brand's presence inside play. The launch media is a separate payment by the brand. It goes to display and video by format (6.1). The price basis is not stated, so the units need confirming.

## Proposal 1

**Text**

Subject: Partnership proposal for Harborline: Tidecall launch

Hello [Name],

Thank you for the time last week. Below is the structure we suggest for one Harborline and Tidecall partnership.

One agreement covers the whole relationship. It has one signature, one timeline, one joint team and one launch calendar.

What Harborline brings: your marks, shown in Tidecall's harbor district, on the in-world signage and on the crew gear. We pay Harborline a license fee for this. The fee is due in two parts, at signing and at launch.

What Tidecall brings: our launch window. Harborline's launch commitment sits in the same agreement. It covers display and video across our network for the first launch period. We set both sides to the same dates, so the marks and the campaign go live on the same day.

Why one deal works better than two: your brand team, our studio and our network team all work from one plan. One approval path covers the marks and the media. If the launch date moves, both parts move together. You do not need to manage a separate media contract.

Next steps:
1. You review the one-page partnership outline.
2. Our legal team sends one draft with the fee schedule and the launch schedule in the same document.
3. Both teams meet for a joint kickoff.

We want Harborline to feel the partnership is one thing, not a list of purchases. Tell us a good time for a call this week.

Regards,
[Name]
Partnerships, [Publisher]

**Fact sheet:** Part A: the publisher pays the brand a license fee. Part B: the brand commits to buy launch display and video, per thousand impressions, no rate stated. The publisher's ad server counts. No outside verifier. No comparison. Part B is a separate payment, not part of the fee and not netted.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Input type: offer. Marks: no native line. License fee: outside the test (6.2). Display and video: placed by format (6.1, first sentence); unplaced as a money commitment until units are named (6.6). "One partnership" did not decide placement. Confirm list: unit and price basis for each, whether the fee offsets the commitment, who counts delivery.

**Grade: Hold.**

## Proposal 2

**Text**

Subject: Tidecall brand license: term sheet summary

Hello [Name],

Here is the summary term sheet for the Harborline brand license in Tidecall.

LICENSE GRANT
Harborline marks appear in Tidecall: signage, vehicles, crew gear and one named in-world location. The territory is worldwide. The term runs from signing to 12 months after launch. The publisher pays Harborline a license fee, in two parts as set out in Schedule 1.

LAUNCH ACTIVATION (Schedule 2)
Every license in our catalog has a launch activation schedule. It supports the licensed marks while the game is new. Under Schedule 2, Harborline commits to display and video placements across our network in the launch window. We book these as activation of the license, and we report them to you in the same partner report as the marks.

APPROVALS
Harborline approves how its marks look. Harborline also approves the launch creative. One approvals contact handles both.

REPORTING
You get one partner report. It covers the live marks and the launch activation together. Placements are priced per thousand impressions, as in the network rate card attached to Schedule 2.

TERMS
Schedules 1 and 2 form one license package. Dates in the two schedules move together.

We treat our license partners as long-term partners. If this structure suits your plan, we can send the full draft after your review.

Regards,
[Name]
Brand Partnerships, [Publisher]

**Fact sheet:** Part A: the publisher pays the brand a license fee. Part B (Schedule 2): the brand buys launch display and video from the publisher's network, per thousand impressions per the rate card. The publisher's ad server counts. No outside counter. No comparison. "Activation of the license" is a label. Part B is a separate media purchase by the brand.

**Judge answer (condensed).** Input type: offer. Marks: no native line. Fee: outside the test (6.2). Schedule 2: display (viewable impressions) and video (completed views) by format (6.1). The money commitment is unplaced (6.6). The label "activation of the license" did not decide. Confirm list: who pays whom in part 3, volumes and amounts, whether "impressions" means viewable, video priced per thousand impressions instead of completed views.

**Grade: Hold.**
