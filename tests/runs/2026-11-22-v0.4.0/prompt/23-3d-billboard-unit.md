## Why

- The seller states the format: a billboard on an object in the game world. This is a standard creative in a slot inside the world. It goes to display (M-01). Rule sentence one places it.
- The seller states the price basis: viewable impression. This is the display price basis (M-01).
- The seller says the venue makes a new unit. The burden is on the seller. The seller must show a change in the price basis (N-6). A new venue does not change the unit (N-2).
- "3D" and "on an object" describe size, placement, or context. These are compared within the line (N-7).
- The seller names published in-game measurement guidelines. A standard that adapts the display price basis to a venue does not create a new price basis (N-8). The exchange check decides (C-3.1). The seller has not stated a different event.
- This is not a brand's presence inside play. A standard creative in a slot is not that item (M-27, "Three places for a brand's presence inside play").
- No route test is needed. F-2 to F-5 do not apply, because F-1 gives the result.

## What to confirm

- Who counts the impressions. Is the counter a party that is not the host?

- Whether the creative is a standard creative in a slot. If the offer is the brand's own 3D object or product placed in the world, it is a different item. Then ask for the route: a currency, a record, or neither (M-27).
- Whether the price is counted on viewable impressions, or on a measure such as attention or time. If it is counted on another measure, the unit is unplaced (N-9).
- The currency, the price terms, and the guarantees. The seller has not stated them.
- The source and method of any attention or engagement claim. Engagement counts are media engagement. No comparison comes with them.
