## 3. What has no native line

The offer is a brand's presence inside play (M-27).

- **Route stated:** neither. The offer states no currency and no record.
- **Currency (N-3, C-3.1):** The offer names three counts: visits, session duration, interactions. It does not say that one defined event is counted the same way by every seller. It does not name a counter that is not the host. These counts are not a currency.
- **Record (N-3, C-3.2):** The offer states no comparison. None of the four requirements is met.
  - R-1: no statement that the venue is held constant.
  - R-2: no statement that the comparison is fixed before the buy.
  - R-3: no statement that the reading covers everyone the buy reached.
  - R-4: no statement that only the difference is the brand's.
- **Where it goes:** the third row of the table for a brand's presence inside play. This is production plus delivery, on partnership terms (M-27).
- **Engagement counts:** Visits, session duration, and interactions are labeled **media engagement**. No comparison comes with them (C-3.2).
- **"Eight minutes":** This is media engagement. It does not show that the brand caused any of those minutes (C-3.2). The question for the seller: "What would this number be with no brand?"
- **"Players love it":** The offer gives no count, no method, and no comparison. It is a claim. It is not a unit.

No verdict on whether the buy works.

## 4. What to confirm

- The unit, the price basis, and the currency for the build (part 1).
- The unit, the price basis, and the terms for the delivery (part 2). The offer states none.
- Whether the offer sets one price for the build and the delivery. If yes, ask for the price of each part (S-6.1).
- Whether the brand pays with its own media or assets. If yes, list them as part of the price (S-6.3).
- Whether any money goes to the brand (S-6.2).
- Whether a standard creative in a slot is in the world. If yes, ask for its format and price basis (M-01).
- How each count is defined: a visit, a session, an interaction.
- Who counts each one. Ask whether it is a party that is not the host.
- How "average eight minutes" is calculated. Ask for the population, the period, and the method.
- Whether any comparison exists: an unbranded twin or another design. Ask whether it is written in the order before the buy.
- The source and method of "players love it".
