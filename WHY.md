# Why: the high-attention claim

## The claim

The case for gaming media rests on one claim more than any other: games are high-attention environments. The IAB's 2026 PlayFronts recap uses these words ("Games are high-attention environments", 25 September 2026).[^1] The IAB's August 2026 guide for marketing leaders advises them to evaluate gaming as "a scaled attention and participation environment" (page 10).[^2] Trade coverage uses the same idea. One article, written with a vendor's voice, gives average in-game engagement times of five to nine minutes per player. It states no sample, period, or method.[^3]

## The claim is accurate for play

People choose to be in a game. They act. They stay. For play, the claim is accurate, and this repository does not dispute it.

## The divide it widens

In most media, the venue's pull is modest. The counts are small views.

In play, the venue's pull is among the strongest in media. The counts are large, and they are behaviors, not views. Large counts of behavior look like brand engagement. But a strong venue can produce much of that behavior with no brand present.

This is a mechanism. It is not a measured finding. This repository does not measure how much of any count belongs to the venue.

## Two directions the claim travels

| Direction | What happens | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| With the label, onto units | The claim attaches to anything sold as gaming, including surfaces with no play. | **Price.** The unit can be priced against the claim, not against the same unit elsewhere. |
| Onto presence inside play, as proof | Minutes, visits, and interactions are offered as evidence that the brand moved people. | **Proof.** Media engagement is read as brand engagement. |

## What is not broken

For standard units, existing lines already compare each unit with the same unit from other sellers. Display compares display. Video compares video. Attention there is a delivery measure, and it is compared within the line.

The test adds no new practice for these units. It only keeps them in their lines.

## What has no rule

A brand's presence inside play has no native line. Engagement counts and surveys do not separate the venue from the brand. A count can be large and true, and still not show what the brand added.

The second sentence of the rule is needed here. A new line must predict the unit, the terms, and the return. It does so by a currency or by a record.

## Why a rule

A label spreads by incentive, not by coordination. Each seller adds the label because buyers ask for it. No one has to plan it, and no one has to mean it.

A rule that sorts by unit works the same way for every seller. It does not depend on anyone's motive. It asks what the unit is, and what it can be compared with.
